Data sources and methodology

Air Alert Stat combines the history of air raid alert signals with researched reports of attacks and civilian consequences. These are two separate processes with different completeness and verification.

1. Alert intervals

  • Kyiv City: Kyiv Digital open data from 25 February 2022; alerts.in.ua from 16 September 2026 as an additional source.
  • Kyiv Oblast: the national air-alert channel via the open ukrainian-air-raid-sirens-dataset from 15 March 2022 to 16 May 2026; the Kyiv Oblast Military Administration Telegram channel from 16 May 2026; alerts.in.ua from 16 September 2026.

Data is grouped into calendar days in Kyiv time. Overlapping reports of the same alert from different sources are merged.

2. How count and duration are calculated

Alert count is the number of alert intervals that started on a calendar day; overlapping intervals are counted once. Alert time is the union of all intervals split at midnight: an alert from 23:30 to 00:40 adds 30 minutes to one day and 40 minutes to the next. Share of time is alert time divided by the length of the period (for the current period, up to the time of calculation). For Kyiv Oblast, oblast-wide, district and community alerts are merged. The dashboard, the monthly archive and the open data all use these rules.

3. Reports of attack consequences

Media publications and official materials serve as evidence for individual consequence records. The system searches for publications automatically, links reports to the date of the event, extracts structured facts and keeps the sources. An incident or casualty count is created only from the text of a publication, never from a headline alone. Later updates can change the description of an earlier event.

4. Reliability and gaps

A day for which automated research has finished is not an independently verified complete register. Zero found reports or intervals do not mean that no attack or alert happened. Casualties are never inferred from indirect signs; unknown stays unknown. Check the sources in each record.

5. Geography and safe display

District markers are administrative aggregates, not strike coordinates. Sources may name a district or a general location, but exact addresses of recent strikes, critical infrastructure and military sites are not published. Uncertain geography is never disguised as precise city-centre coordinates.

6. Using the statistics

To analyse trends, compare equal periods and take source coverage into account. Do not compare the number of media reports with the number of alerts as if they were the same measure. The data is for information and research, not a warning system. For safety decisions, rely on official alert signals and instructions.

7. Transparency

The state of automated processing, errors and the queue of historical dates are shown on the progress page. The code and architecture are published in an open repository.
